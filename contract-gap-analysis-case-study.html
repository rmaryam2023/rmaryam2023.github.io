AI-Powered Contract Gap Analysis Platform

Timeline 3 months
Impact 80% labor reduction

The Challenge

ThreatConnect's legal and compliance teams were facing a critical bottleneck. As the company scaled and expanded into new jurisdictions (UK, EU, India, APAC), every new client contract required exhaustive manual review to ensure compliance with region-specific data protection laws—GDPR, DPDP Act, PIPEDA, and dozens more. This process was:

  • Dangerously slow: 330 minutes (5.5 hours) per contract review cycle—from intake to finalization
  • Expensive: $465 in labor costs per contract at $80/hour legal rates
  • Error-prone: Manual identification of privacy restrictions and cross-border transfer mechanisms missed critical clauses
  • Non-scalable: Legal team bandwidth couldn't keep pace with sales pipeline velocity
  • High-risk: Missed compliance requirements could expose the company to regulatory penalties and audit failures
Core Problem: How do we automate jurisdiction detection, clause extraction, and compliance gap analysis across 40+ international privacy frameworks while eliminating AI hallucinations that could provide bad legal advice?

The Solution

I architected a serverless compliance automation platform on AWS that combines rule-based logic with constrained AI to eliminate hallucinations while achieving 80% labor reduction. The system is designed around a critical principle: AI generates answers only from verified data sources we control—not from freely searching the internet or inventing clauses.

Design Philosophy: Rules + AI, Not AI Alone

Instead of asking an LLM to "figure out compliance," I built a hybrid system where structured rules handle deterministic logic (jurisdiction detection, clause matching) and AI handles nuanced tasks (clause extraction, similarity analysis, plain-language explanations). This architecture costs under $200/month and produces predictable, auditable outputs.

Architecture Overview

The system operates in 7 automated stages:

Stage 1: Document Intake via SharePoint Integration

Sales teams upload Master Service Agreements (MSAs) to a designated SharePoint folder. I built an AWS Lambda function triggered by SharePoint webhooks that automatically:

  • Pulls the PDF from SharePoint into S3 bucket (msa-intake-mr-2026)
  • Captures metadata: customer name, country, contract value, upload date
  • Initiates the compliance pipeline

Stage 2: Text Extraction with AWS Textract

ExtractMSAText Lambda function processes uploaded PDFs using AWS Textract:

  • Handles multi-page contracts with asynchronous API for files >5MB
  • Extracts structured text while preserving document layout context
  • Outputs JSON to processed-output-mr-2026/msa-text/

This same pattern is used for regulation documents uploaded by legal team—ExtractRegulationText Lambda extracts text from GDPR, DPDP Act, CCPA PDFs into a compliance library.

Stage 3: Jurisdiction & Compliance Detection (AWS Comprehend)

AnalyzeCompliance Lambda uses pattern matching and AWS Comprehend to detect:

  • Jurisdiction: "England and Wales," "Delaware," "governed by UK law" → triggers UK compliance rules
  • Data protection regime: Scans for trigger terms like "Data Controller," "GDPR," "Personal Data," "Standard Contractual Clauses"
  • Transfer requirements: Identifies cross-border data transfer mechanisms

Example: Detects "India + DPDP Act" → queries DynamoDB for India-specific rules (24-hour breach notification, data localization, CERT-In requirements)

Stage 4: Rule-Based Compliance Checking (DynamoDB + Lambda)

Instead of asking AI to "reason" about compliance, I stored structured rules in DynamoDB: 

{
  "Jurisdiction": "UK",
  "ClauseType": "DataBreachNotification",
  "RequiredText": "notify Customer within 36 hours",
  "Threshold": "36",
  "RiskLevel": "HIGH",
  "Regulation": "UK GDPR Article 33"
}

The system compares extracted MSA clauses against required clause sets and generates: "MISSING," "NON-COMPLIANT," or "NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW" with specific evidence citations.

Stage 5: AI-Assisted Clause Extraction (AWS Bedrock)

ExtractRulesAI Lambda uses AWS Bedrock (with Claude or GPT models) for tasks that benefit from NLP:

  • Extracting clause text from regulation documents using pattern matching + semantic search
  • Identifying similar clauses across different contract templates
  • Generating plain-language explanations: "Why is UK SCC required? UK GDPR Chapter V requires lawful transfer mechanisms for data sent outside UK..."

Key constraint: AI prompts are tightly scoped with few-shot examples to prevent hallucinations. All AI outputs are verified against source documents.

Stage 6: Automated Report Generation & SharePoint Return

The compliance report is generated as a structured JSON file containing:

  • Executive Summary: Compliance rate, high-risk issues count
  • Detailed Findings: Clause-by-clause analysis with evidence citations
  • Recommendations: Specific actions ("Add UK SCC fallback to Section 6.3")
  • Missing Clauses: Pre-approved clause text ready for insertion

A Word document with tracked changes is auto-generated and returned to SharePoint, where legal can accept/modify/reject suggested clauses.

Stage 7: Workflow Automation & Analytics

Built a monitoring dashboard that tracks:

  • Upload → Review → Approval lifecycle timing
  • Clause rejection rate (identifies clauses that need refinement)
  • Most common compliance gaps by jurisdiction

Output Example:

✓ IP Ownership – COMPLIANT
⚠ Data Breach Notification – 72h (UK requires 36h) 
✗ International Transfer Mechanism – MISSING (needs UK SCCs or Data Bridge)

Technical Deep Dive

AWS Architecture: Serverless Event-Driven Pipeline

Core AWS Services:

  • S3 Buckets: msa-intake-mr-2026, compliance-rules-mr-2026, processed-output-mr-2026
  • Lambda Functions: 4 functions with event-driven triggers (S3 upload events, Lambda invocations)
  • DynamoDB: complianceRulesTable with Partition Key: Jurisdiction, Sort Key: ClauseType
  • AWS Textract: Async API for multi-page PDFs (handles 100+ page contracts)
  • AWS Comprehend: Entity extraction and key phrase detection
  • AWS Bedrock: Claude/GPT models for clause extraction and explanations

Lambda Function Architecture

1. ExtractMSAText (512MB, 5min timeout)

  • Trigger: S3 upload to msa-intake-mr-2026
  • Uses synchronous Textract for PDFs <5MB, async for larger files
  • Implements polling loop with 5-second intervals (max 5min timeout)
  • Extracts text while preserving line breaks and structure
  • Automatically invokes AnalyzeCompliance on completion

2. AnalyzeCompliance (1024MB, 5min timeout)

  • Invoked by ExtractMSAText
  • Pattern matching for jurisdiction detection (40+ indicators)
  • DynamoDB queries to fetch jurisdiction-specific rules
  • Regex-based clause detection with confidence scoring
  • Generates compliance report JSON with evidence citations

3. ExtractRegulationText (512MB, 5min timeout)

  • Trigger: S3 upload to compliance-rules-mr-2026
  • Processes regulation PDFs (GDPR, DPDP Act, CCPA, etc.)
  • Handles scanned documents with OCR
  • Outputs structured JSON to processed-output-mr-2026/regulations/

4. ExtractRulesAI (512MB, 5min timeout)

  • Trigger: JSON upload to processed-output-mr-2026/regulations/
  • NLP pattern matching for clause extraction (breach notification timeframes, security requirements, etc.)
  • Stores extracted rules in DynamoDB with metadata (risk level, regulation article)
  • Automatically populates compliance library

IAM Security Configuration

Created LambdaComplianceCheckerRole with least-privilege permissions:

  • S3: GetObject, PutObject, ListBucket on 3 specific buckets
  • Textract: Full access for document analysis
  • Comprehend: Full access for entity extraction
  • DynamoDB: Read/Write on complianceRulesTable
  • Lambda: InvokeFunction permission for AnalyzeCompliance
  • CloudWatch: Logs for monitoring and debugging

SharePoint Integration Strategy

Built a bidirectional integration between SharePoint and AWS:

  • Inbound: SharePoint webhook → API Gateway → Lambda → S3. Sales teams upload directly to SharePoint; system automatically pulls files into AWS pipeline
  • Outbound: Lambda generates DOCX with tracked changes → uploads to SharePoint output folder. Legal team reviews in familiar interface
  • Metadata sync: Contract metadata (customer, country, status) bidirectionally synced between SharePoint lists and DynamoDB

Prompt Engineering for Legal Accuracy

Developed multi-stage prompting to constrain AI behavior:

  • Stage 1 - Extraction: "You are a legal analyst. Extract ONLY the data breach notification clause from this contract. Quote the exact text. Do not paraphrase."
  • Stage 2 - Classification: Few-shot examples with 5 known good/bad clauses to establish pattern
  • Stage 3 - Verification: Separate LLM call validates extracted text exists in source document (anti-hallucination check)
  • Stage 4 - Explanation: "Explain why UK GDPR Article 33 requires 36-hour notification in 2 sentences, citing specific article text"

Critical constraint: AI is never asked to make compliance decisions. It only extracts, classifies, and explains. Rules engine makes final determinations.

Cost Optimization

Kept monthly costs under $200 through:

  • S3 Intelligent-Tiering for processed documents
  • DynamoDB On-Demand billing (only pay for queries)
  • Lambda ephemeral storage instead of EFS
  • Textract batching for bulk document processing
  • Caching common regulation text (GDPR, CCPA) to avoid repeated extraction
  • Using AWS Bedrock with smaller models (Claude Haiku) for simple extraction tasks

Challenges & Learnings

Challenge 1: Preventing AI Hallucinations in Legal Context

Early prototypes using pure LLM reasoning would occasionally cite clauses that didn't exist or invent compliance requirements. This is catastrophic in legal work—bad advice could expose the company to regulatory penalties.

Solution:

  • Shifted from "AI reasoning" to "AI extraction + rule-based logic"
  • Required all clause references to include exact text quotes and character positions
  • Implemented verification Lambda that cross-checks AI outputs against source documents
  • Added confidence scores: anything <80% is flagged for human review
  • Stored all compliance rules as structured data in DynamoDB (not LLM memory)

Result: Zero hallucination incidents in production across 1000+ contracts

Challenge 2: Handling Jurisdiction Complexity

Contracts don't always clearly state governing law. Some have dual jurisdiction clauses ("UK for data protection, Delaware for commercial disputes"), others reference outdated frameworks (EU-US Privacy Shield post-Schrems II), and emerging markets have inconsistent terminology.

Solution:

  • Built 40+ jurisdiction detection patterns (not just simple keyword matching)
  • Implemented confidence scoring: "90% UK, 10% EU" → triggers both rule sets
  • Created fallback logic: if jurisdiction unclear, apply strictest requirements (GDPR + CCPA union)
  • Added manual override capability for edge cases

Challenge 3: Textract Accuracy on Scanned/Low-Quality PDFs

Some older contracts were scanned PDFs with poor OCR quality. Textract would misread "36 hours" as "3B hours" or skip tables entirely.

Solution:

  • Preprocessing step that detects scanned PDFs (checks for embedded text layer)
  • Routes low-quality docs through enhanced OCR pipeline with post-processing cleanup
  • Regex validation: "3B hours" fails numeric validation → flags document for manual review
  • Table extraction using Textract's table detection API for structured compliance data

Challenge 4: SharePoint Integration Authentication

SharePoint webhook integration required OAuth 2.0 authentication with token refresh logic. Initial implementation had intermittent failures when tokens expired mid-upload.

Solution:

  • Implemented AWS Secrets Manager to store SharePoint credentials
  • Lambda function automatically refreshes OAuth tokens before expiration
  • Added retry logic with exponential backoff for transient SharePoint API errors
  • Created dead-letter queue (SQS) to capture failed uploads for manual retry

Challenge 5: Cost Management at Scale

Initial cost projections were $50/month. At 50+ contracts per month, costs ballooned to $600/month due to repeated Textract calls and oversized Lambda functions.

Optimizations:

  • Caching: Store extracted text for 30 days (many contracts have minor amendments → reuse base text)
  • Lambda right-sizing: Reduced memory allocation from 2048MB to 512MB for most functions
  • Textract batching: Group similar documents for bulk processing during off-peak hours
  • DynamoDB On-Demand instead of Provisioned: Only pay for actual queries
  • S3 Intelligent-Tiering: Automatically moves old reports to cheaper storage

Result: Stabilized costs at $180-200/month even with 100+ contracts monthly

What I'd Do Differently

Reflecting on the project, here are areas for improvement in v2:

  • Start with rules, not AI: Initial prototype relied too heavily on LLM reasoning. Should have built the DynamoDB rule engine first, then added AI for edge cases.
  • Invest in UI earlier: Legal team struggled with JSON reports. v2 includes a web dashboard with visual diff viewer and one-click clause insertion.
  • More granular privacy framework support: Currently treats "US" as one jurisdiction. v2 breaks down into state-level compliance (CCPA, VCDPA, CTDPA separately).
  • Versioning for regulations: GDPR evolves, DPDP Act gets amended. Need versioned compliance rules with effective dates.
  • User feedback loop: When legal team rejects a clause recommendation, capture the reason and auto-update rules.

Key Takeaways

For building AI compliance systems:

  • Constrain AI aggressively. In legal/regulatory contexts, AI should extract and explain—never reason or decide. Use structured rules for deterministic logic.
  • Verification layers are non-negotiable. Every AI output must be validated against source documents. Confidence scores below 80% go to human review.
  • Event-driven serverless scales beautifully. Lambda + S3 triggers handled 0→100 contracts/month with zero infrastructure changes.
  • SharePoint integration matters. Legal teams live in Office 365. Meeting them where they are (vs. forcing new tools) drove adoption.
  • Start narrow, expand systematically. Launched with UK GDPR only, then added jurisdictions one-by-one after validating accuracy.
  • Cost optimization is continuous. Initial architecture was 3x over budget. Caching, right-sizing, and batching brought costs down 70%.
  • Legal stakeholders are your best QA. Weekly review sessions with legal team caught edge cases that no amount of testing would find.
